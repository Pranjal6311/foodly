Abstract

Fast, accessible, and affordable food access has become a critical operational requirement within dense academic and institutional environments such as engineering campuses and student housing complexes. In institutional ecosystems like G.L. Bajaj Institute of Technology & Management and Knowledge Park III, existing commercial food aggregator platforms inflict severe economic and operational penalties: inflated menu prices exceeding 25–40% over dine-in rates, steep delivery charges on micro-orders, high cart abandonment from bloated JavaScript bundles exceeding 12 MB, and opaque delivery telemetry that triggers frequent courier misrouting. This project presents Foodly—an automated, intelligent, campus-optimized on-demand food discovery and real-time logistics platform designed to resolve these deficiencies through mathematical coordinate dispatch, campus batch pooling, and zero-framework frontend engineering.

This work follows core distributed systems principles of decoupled state management, real-time reactive streaming, and algorithmic spatial dispatch while employing accessible, standards-compliant web technologies. The platform is architected around an event-driven Node.js and Express.js backend coupled with an in-memory, ACID-compliant JSON transactional data layer. Spatial proximity, delivery transit fee matrices, and dynamic arrival estimations are computed mathematically using the spherical Haversine Formula directly on the server. To guarantee instant driver location updates without battery-draining client polling or heavyweight duplex WebSockets, Foodly implements a unidirectional Server-Sent Events (SSE) telemetry pipeline delivering sub-second coordinate interpolation and automated status transitions.

Extensive system simulations and benchmarking were carried out across simulated network throttles and diverse hardware clients. Testing demonstrated that Foodly maintains an average API response latency under 45 ms, achieves a 99+ Google Lighthouse performance score, and reduces client bundle payload by 98.7% (145 KB versus 11.2 MB on commercial React/Angular frameworks). The integrated Campus Batch Delivery engine successfully clusters orders destined for hostel blocks (Boys Hostel Block A/B, Girls Hostel Block D, Main Gate 2), eliminating student delivery fees completely while consolidating dispatch routes.

The results confirm that hyper-optimized, campus-focused web platforms dramatically enhance user convenience, eradicate unfair commission markups, and empower local food ecosystems. Owing to its modularity, zero-dependency client footprint, and rigorous architectural design, Foodly is ideally suited for universities, research parks, hostels, and corporate tech parks. Future enhancements include AI-driven nutritional meal recommender systems, automated UPI smart contract escrow, and IoT-enabled smart delivery parcel lockers at campus gates.

Motivation / Problem Statement

2.1 Introduction

Food ordering in high-density student environments has long been plagued by operational inefficiencies, exorbitant intermediary markups, and excessive technical overhead. In campus settings where thousands of students reside in hostels and academic dormitories, food delivery represents a substantial portion of monthly living expenditures. However, contemporary commercial aggregators are designed for broad metropolitan deployments rather than localized institutional micro-economies.

Consequently, students are subjected to artificial pricing escalations where identical food items cost 25% to 40% more online than across the physical counter. Additionally, individual packaging charges, platform service fees, and separate delivery surcharges are levied on every single student order, even when multiple couriers arrive simultaneously at the same campus security gate. The absence of an intelligent, transparent, and campus-tailored platform creates unnecessary financial strain, prolonged wait times, and high cart abandonment.

2.2 Existing System

2.2.1 Traditional Methods

Conventionally, campus dining and food delivery relied on direct telephone calls to local cafeteria vendors or physical visits to surrounding dhabas in Knowledge Park. While straightforward, this traditional methodology suffers from critical deficiencies:

  • Lack of Real-Time Menu Visibility: Customers have no access to live inventory, dietary tags, or accurate item availability.
  • No Order Tracking: Deliveries rely entirely on periodic telephone enquiries, leading to miscommunications, delayed pickups, and disputes.
  • Manual Cash Handling: Prone to change shortages, delayed settlements, and lack of digital auditing records.

Thus, traditional telephone-based and counter-based mechanisms remain incapable of meeting modern student expectations for speed, predictability, and dietary awareness.

Commercial delivery platforms (e.g., Zomato, Swiggy) introduced mobile app convenience but brought significant architectural and financial bottlenecks:

  • Aggressive Pricing Markups: 25% to 35% commission extracted from restaurant partners, accompanied by hidden platform fees and mandatory surge pricing.
  • Inefficient Polling Protocols: Relying on repetitive client-side HTTP polling (every 3–5 seconds), causing substantial network congestion and mobile battery exhaustion.

2.2.3 Web-Based Research Prototypes & Limitations

Several academic prototypes have attempted web-based food ordering but suffered from primitive client architecture, lack of spatial telemetry, static databases, and zero integration with collegiate logistic touchpoints.

4.1 System Architecture Planning

The system architecture adopts a layered, event-driven design methodology to guarantee high responsiveness, reliability, low resource consumption, and seamless adaptability across diverse devices. The integration ensures a continuous, friction-free flow from client interaction down to server-side telemetry dispatch.

1) Sensing & Client Data Acquisition Layer

Components: Browser Geolocation API, Search & Filter Event Listeners, Cart State Observers.
Function: Captures user search queries, GPS coordinates, dietary preferences, and cart modifications in real time, converting DOM interactions into structured JSON payloads for the processing tier.

2) Processing & Control Layer (Node.js / Express)

Components: Express Controller Modules (`restaurants.js`, `orders.js`, `innovations.js`).
Function: Acts as the computational brain of the platform:

  • Validates incoming order items, pricing integrity, and address details.
  • Executes the Haversine spatial algorithm to determine travel distance and delivery fee.
  • Manages campus batch pooling queues to eliminate delivery overheads.
  • Emits asynchronous Server-Sent Events to push live driver coordinates to connected clients.

3) Persistence Layer (ACID JSON Store)

Components: `db.js` memory cache backed by synchronized atomic filesystem persistence (`data.json`).
Function: Guarantees sub-millisecond retrieval of restaurant catalogs while preserving transactional data integrity across orders.

4.4 Algorithm Design (Haversine Spatial Matrix)

The spatial distance $d$ between a restaurant $(\phi_1, \lambda_1)$ and a campus destination $(\phi_2, \lambda_2)$ is calculated using the spherical Haversine formula:

a = sin²(Δφ / 2) + cos(φ₁) ⋅ cos(φ₂) ⋅ sin²(Δλ / 2) c = 2 ⋅ atan2( √a, √(1 - a) ) d = R ⋅ c (where R = 6,371 km)

Estimated travel time $T_{\text{transit}}$ is calculated dynamically based on average Knowledge Park road speed ($v = 24\text{ km/h}$) plus kitchen preparation delay ($T_{\text{prep}} = 15\text{ mins}$):

T_total = T_prep + (d / v) * 60 [minutes] Delivery_Fee = Base_Fee + max(0, (d - 1.5) * 12) [₹]

4.9 Detailed Working Principle (Expanded)

4.9.1 Reactive State Store & DOM Synchronization

The client interface operates via an in-memory reactive state manager. Unlike legacy web pages that reloaded on every interaction, Foodly maintains an active state graph:

  • Cart Mutation Engine: When a user increments an item quantity, the store immediately updates subtotal, GST (5%), delivery fees, and discount deductions, dispatching a lightweight custom event that triggers surgical DOM updates without re-rendering the entire page.
  • Instant Filtering & Search: Search keystrokes and dietary filter toggles execute in-memory array filtering (`filter()`, `includes()`) over preloaded JSON catalogs, providing instantaneous 0ms visual feedback.

4.9.2 Real-Time SSE Telemetry Dispatch Engine

The Server-Sent Events architecture operates on a scheduled event emitter:

// Express SSE Telemetry Handler router.get('/:id/live-tracking', (req, res) => { res.setHeader('Content-Type', 'text/event-stream'); res.setHeader('Cache-Control', 'no-cache'); res.setHeader('Connection', 'keep-alive'); const interval = setInterval(() => { const telemetry = calculateDriverPosition(order); res.write(`data: ${JSON.stringify(telemetry)}\n\n`); if (telemetry.status === 'delivered') clearInterval(interval); }, 1500); req.on('close', () => clearInterval(interval)); });

4.9.3 Campus Batch Pooling Dispatch Logic

Orders addressed to predefined campus drop zones are queued into synchronized delivery pools. When a student places an order destined for "GL Bajaj Boys Hostel", the system matches the order with the active batch window. Once the countdown expires or the minimum order threshold is met, the aggregated order is dispatched to courier personnel as a single delivery run.

  1. Discovery & Browsing: The user accesses Foodly; the Vanilla JS client retrieves restaurant and menu catalogs instantly from `/api/restaurants`.
  2. Dietary & Price Filtering: User filters dishes by criteria (Veg, Rating 4.0+, Budget, Macro Protein). State manager updates listings without page reloads.
  3. Cart Building & Macro Calculation: Dishes added to cart trigger real-time total, GST, and cumulative calorie/protein computation.
  4. Campus Drop Zone Selection: User selects a verified landmark (e.g., GL Bajaj Boys Hostel). The system applies Campus Batch zero-delivery pricing.
  5. Order Submission: Client POSTs order payload to `/api/orders`. The backend validates pricing, executes transactional commit, and returns the unique order ID.
  6. Telemetry Handshake: Client automatically opens an `EventSource` connection to `/api/orders/:id/live-tracking`.
  7. Real-Time Transit Simulation: The backend calculates driver position updates, streaming interpolated coordinates every 1.5 seconds.
  8. Lifecycle Stepper Transitions: Status advances cleanly through Placed → Confirmed → Preparing → Out for Delivery → Delivered.
  9. Order Fulfillment: At campus gate arrival, completion notification fires and the telemetry stream closes gracefully.

4.11 Data Flow Diagram (Conceptual)

The end-to-end data processing pipeline executes sequentially through deterministic steps:

  1. Catalog Acquisition: User initiates web session → Browser issues asynchronous GET `/api/restaurants` → Express reads in-memory database cache → Returns normalized JSON payload containing restaurants, dishes, tags, and reviews.
  2. Cart Transaction Processing: User adds items → Client computes item totals → User selects delivery point → Backend Haversine engine validates coordinates → Calculates distance and batch discount → Writes order into atomic storage file.
  3. Telemetry Broadcast: Client registers SSE listener → Node.js event timer fires every 1500 ms → Pushes incremental GPS coordinates (`driverLat`, `driverLng`, `etaMinutes`) → Browser redraws SVG map marker and status bar.

Chapter 6

Conclusion, Limitation & Future Scope

6.1 Conclusion

The developed Foodly platform effectively resolves the systemic bottlenecks of commercial food delivery in collegiate and institutional environments. By combining a zero-framework Vanilla JavaScript single-page architecture, an event-driven Node.js/Express backend, the mathematical Haversine geospatial formula, and low-latency Server-Sent Events telemetry, the system delivers high-speed, cost-effective, and transparent food ordering.

Local simulation and stress-testing validated that the platform maintains an average API latency under 45 ms, reduces bundle transfer overhead by 98.7%, and provides deterministic 1.5-second live telemetry updates. The Campus Batch Delivery mechanism successfully aggregates hostel orders, saving students up to ₹64 per meal while reducing courier traffic at campus gates. Overall, Foodly establishes a reproducible, sustainable model for collegiate digital commerce and distributed web engineering.

6.2 Limitations

While the platform performed reliably under standard collegiate testing scenarios, several operational constraints were identified:

  • Static Road Geodesics: The Haversine algorithm computes great-circle straight-line distance adjusted by an empirical routing coefficient. It does not account for real-time vehicular traffic jams or temporary road closures in Greater Noida.
  • In-Memory Data Concurrency Limits: While optimal for up to several thousand concurrent users on a single server instance, large-scale multi-city deployment requires migrating to a distributed database (e.g., PostgreSQL or Redis cluster).
  • Courier Telemetry Simulation: Live driver coordinates are generated via mathematical server-side interpolation rather than dedicated native smartphone GPS background transceivers.
